# Control 3.10: SEC Reg S-P -- Privacy of Consumer Financial Information

**Control ID:** 3.10
**Pillar:** Compliance & Audit
**Regulatory Reference:** SEC Regulation S-P (17 CFR 248.30), GLBA Title V (Privacy), SEC Regulation S-ID (Identity Theft Red Flags)
**Last Verified:** 2026-08-24
**Governance Levels:** Baseline / Recommended / Regulated

---

## Objective

Establish controls governing Microsoft 365 Copilot's access to consumer financial information, helping meet SEC Regulation S-P privacy requirements for safeguarding nonpublic personal information (NPI), maintaining privacy notice accuracy, supporting opt-out provisions, and implementing the safeguard requirements specified under the Safeguards Rule (Rule 248.30) -- including the amendments proposed in 2023 and adopted by the SEC in May 2024 (Release No. 34-100155), whose tiered compliance dates had passed as of August 24, 2026.

## Why This Matters for FSI

SEC Regulation S-P implements the privacy provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) Title V for SEC-registered broker-dealers, investment advisers, and investment companies. In broker-dealer workflows, NPI generally flows from the consumer or customer to the broker-dealer that receives, stores, and processes it; Copilot controls should focus on safeguarding that customer information inside Microsoft 365 rather than describing a broker-to-consumer data transfer. The regulation requires financial institutions to:

1. **Provide privacy notices** to customers describing information-sharing practices
2. **Offer opt-out rights** before sharing NPI with nonaffiliated third parties
3. **Implement safeguards** to protect the security and confidentiality of customer records and information

**Reg S-P 2024 amendments (compliance dates passed):** In May 2024, the SEC adopted amendments to Regulation S-P in [Release No. 34-100155](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-100155.pdf). The final rule's Table 3 set tiered *former* compliance dates; the classifications below do not limit which covered institutions are subject to the rule:

| Table 3 classification | Qualification | Former compliance date |
|------------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| Investment companies together with their group of related investment companies | Net assets of **$1 billion or more** at the end of the most recent fiscal year | December 3, 2025 |
| Registered investment advisers | **$1.5 billion or more in assets under management** | December 3, 2025 |
| Broker-dealers | Not a small entity under the Securities Exchange Act Regulatory Flexibility Act definition. A broker-dealer is small only if it had total capital below $500,000 at the applicable prior-fiscal-year measurement date **and** is not affiliated with a person that is not a small entity. | December 3, 2025 |
| Transfer agents | Not a small entity under the applicable Exchange Act Regulatory Flexibility Act definition. A transfer agent is small only if it received fewer than 500 transfer items and fewer than 500 processing items in the preceding six months, handled only issuers that are small entities, maintained fewer than 1,000 aggregate shareholder accounts or was named transfer agent for fewer than 1,000 accounts throughout the prior fiscal year, **and** is not affiliated with a person that is not a small entity. | December 3, 2025 |
| Other covered institutions | Does not meet a Table 3 larger-entity qualification | June 3, 2026 |

As of **August 24, 2026**, both December 3, 2025 and June 3, 2026 compliance dates have passed. Institutions should use their own facts and applicable small-entity definition to document the former Table 3 classification and promptly address any unimplemented amended-rule requirements.

Microsoft 365 Copilot presents specific Reg S-P challenges because it accesses and processes NPI stored across Microsoft 365 -- customer account data in emails, financial information in SharePoint documents, client records in Teams conversations, and personal details in OneDrive files. Copilot's ability to surface, summarize, and generate content from NPI creates new vectors for potential unauthorized disclosure or inadequate safeguarding that must be addressed within the Reg S-P compliance framework.

## Control Description

This control addresses the intersection of Copilot functionality with Reg S-P requirements across all three pillars: privacy notices, opt-out provisions, and safeguards. The amended Reg S-P requirements (incident response program and 72-hour notification) are integrated throughout.

### Reg S-P Amendment Details: Response, Affected-Individual Notice, and Service Providers

The [final rule text for 17 CFR 248.30(a)(3)–(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-248/section-248.30) assigns distinct responsibilities that an incident workflow must not collapse:

**SEC Rule 248.30(a)(3) — Response program for unauthorized access to or use of customer information:** Written safeguards policies and procedures must include a program reasonably designed to detect, respond to, and recover from unauthorized access to or use of customer information, including customer-notification procedures. The program assesses the incident's nature and scope, contains and controls it, and applies the affected-individual process in paragraph (a)(4).

**SEC Rule 248.30(a)(4) — Notifying affected individuals:** Unless the covered institution makes the rule's reasonable-investigation determination that sensitive customer information has not been, and is not reasonably likely to be, used in a manner that would result in substantial harm or inconvenience, it must notify affected individuals. If required, notice is due as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days after the covered institution becomes aware that unauthorized access to or use has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred.

**SEC Rule 248.30(a)(5) — Service-provider oversight and provider-to-institution notice:** The response program must include written policies and procedures for service-provider oversight, including due diligence and monitoring. Those policies and procedures must be reasonably designed to ensure that a service provider protects customer information and notifies the covered institution as soon as possible, but no later than 72 hours after **the service provider becomes aware** of a security breach resulting in unauthorized access to a customer-information system the provider maintains. On receipt, the covered institution must initiate its paragraph (a)(3) response program. The final rule does not impose a general written-contract requirement for this duty; it permits, but does not require, a written agreement for a provider to notify affected individuals on the institution's behalf, while the institution retains that obligation.

### Reg S-P Safeguards Rule Requirements and Copilot Controls

| Safeguards Rule Requirement | Copilot Control |
|---------------------------|-----------------|
| Designate a qualified individual to oversee the safeguards program | Include Copilot in the designated individual's scope of responsibility |
| Conduct risk assessments of customer information | Assess Copilot as a system that accesses and processes customer information |
| Design and implement safeguards to control identified risks | Implement DLP, sensitivity labels, information barriers, and access controls for Copilot |
| Regularly test safeguard effectiveness | Include Copilot-specific test scenarios in safeguard testing program |
| Train personnel on safeguard policies | Include Copilot NPI access risks in security awareness training |
| Oversee service provider arrangements | Review Microsoft's data processing practices for Copilot NPI access |
| Establish a response program (Rule 248.30(a)(3)) | Maintain a written Copilot-aware program for detection, response, recovery, scope assessment, containment, and notification procedures |
| Notify affected individuals (Rule 248.30(a)(4)) | Separately investigate the notification determination and, when notice is required, track the institution's affected-individual notice clock |
| Oversee service providers and receive provider notice (Rule 248.30(a)(5)) | Perform due diligence and monitoring; evidence provider awareness time, institution receipt time, reported scope, and the provider-to-institution 72-hour timing evaluation |

### Privacy Notice Considerations

When Copilot accesses NPI, the firm's privacy notice must accurately describe:

- **Categories of NPI collected and used:** Privacy notices should reflect that AI tools access NPI for operational purposes
- **Parties with whom NPI is shared:** If Copilot processing involves Microsoft as a service provider, this should be reflected in service provider disclosures
- **Security practices:** The notice should reference AI-related safeguards as part of the firm's security program
- **Consumer rights:** Opt-out rights and procedures should be clearly stated

## Governance Levels

### Baseline

- Record the correct final-rule mapping: Rule 248.30(a)(3) is the response program, (a)(4) is affected-individual notice, and (a)(5) is service-provider oversight and provider-to-institution notice. Apply Table 3's $1 billion investment-company-group net-assets threshold, $1.5 billion RIA AUM threshold, and applicable broker-dealer/transfer-agent RFA small-entity definitions; both former compliance dates had passed by August 24, 2026.
- Assess Copilot as a system that accesses NPI in the firm's Reg S-P risk assessment
- Verify DLP policies cover NPI data types (SSN, account numbers, financial data) across Copilot-accessible locations
- Review privacy notices for accuracy regarding AI tool usage and NPI access
- Include Copilot NPI access in the firm's information security program documentation
- Establish the written Rule 248.30(a)(3) response program for Copilot-related NPI incidents and separately document the Rule 248.30(a)(4) affected-individual process and Rule 248.30(a)(5) service-provider oversight and intake process
- Enable current Data Security Posture Management (**Purview portal > Solutions > DSPM**) and review **Discover > Activity explorer > AI activities** for SITs detected in Copilot prompts and responses; access to prompt and response bodies requires the additional content-viewer roles documented by Microsoft
- Train Copilot users on NPI handling obligations and Copilot-specific NPI risks

### Recommended

- Implement sensitivity labels for NPI-containing files and emails and use the Copilot DLP action that prevents labeled content from being processed; the item can still appear as a citation
- Configure information barriers where supported to prevent cross-segment access (for example, advisory vs. brokerage). Information Barriers don't support the SharePoint Embedded content used by Copilot Pages and Copilot Notebooks
- Deploy a Custom DLP policy for the **Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Chat** location. Use separate rules for sensitivity-label content exclusion, SIT-based external web-search restriction, and -- only after confirming preview rollout in the tenant -- SIT-based prompt blocking. Assess the preview external-email exclusion rule separately. DLP doesn't scan the contents of files uploaded directly into prompts
- Use current DSPM (**Purview portal > Solutions > DSPM**) AI observability and **Discover > Activity explorer > AI activities** to review sensitive interactions, DLP rule matches, and oversharing indicators
- Conduct annual Reg S-P risk assessment with explicit Copilot NPI analysis
- Implement automated NPI detection and classification for content accessed by Copilot
- Operate Rule 248.30(a)(5) due diligence and monitoring for applicable service providers. Test intake of a provider-to-institution notice without asserting that the final rule requires a written contract; retain the provider awareness time, institution receipt time, provider-reported scope, and 72-hour timing evaluation.
- Test incident response procedures with Copilot-related NPI breach scenarios
- Update privacy notices to explicitly address AI tool usage in NPI processing
- Monitor Copilot access patterns for anomalous NPI access

### Regulated

- Monitor Copilot interactions through Purview Audit, DSPM AI activities, and Copilot DLP alerts. If Microsoft Sentinel is used, validate the ingestion pipeline and alert latency before describing it as real-time; the built-in Microsoft 365 connector documentation lists Exchange, SharePoint, and Teams audit workloads and doesn't by itself establish Copilot audit ingestion
- Configure DLP alerts for the supported Copilot rule matches involving high-sensitivity NPI (SSNs, financial account numbers)
- Conduct quarterly NPI access reviews for Copilot-licensed users
- Implement data minimization controls to limit NPI exposure through Copilot
- Implement the amended Reg S-P incident workflow: (1) detection, response, and recovery under Rule 248.30(a)(3); (2) scope determination using validated Purview evidence; (3) service-provider oversight and provider-to-institution intake under Rule 248.30(a)(5), starting the 72-hour evaluation from provider awareness; and (4) the separate affected-individual determination and notice process under Rule 248.30(a)(4)
- Prepare Reg S-P examination-ready documentation including Copilot safeguards, the Rule 248.30(a)(3) response program, Rule 248.30(a)(4) investigation/notice records, and Rule 248.30(a)(5) provider oversight and timing evidence
- Conduct tabletop exercises that keep the provider-to-institution 72-hour timing evaluation separate from the institution's response-program actions and any affected-individual notification clock
- Commission independent assessment of Copilot NPI safeguard effectiveness
- Implement continuous compliance monitoring for Reg S-P safeguard requirements across Copilot surfaces

## Setup & Configuration

### Step 1: Assess Copilot NPI Access Scope

1. Inventory NPI data types stored across Microsoft 365 workloads:
    - Exchange: Client emails, account statements, application forms
    - SharePoint: Client files, KYC documents, financial records
    - OneDrive: Individual files containing NPI
    - Teams: Client conversations, meeting transcripts
2. Map Copilot access to each NPI location based on user permissions
3. Identify high-risk NPI access scenarios:
    - Copilot Chat queries that could surface NPI from multiple sources
    - Copilot summaries that aggregate NPI from multiple documents
    - Copilot-drafted communications that reference NPI
4. Document findings in the firm's Reg S-P risk assessment

### Step 2: Configure DLP Policies for NPI Protection

1. Navigate to [Microsoft Purview portal](https://purview.microsoft.com)
2. Go to **Data Loss Prevention** > **Policies**
3. Create or update DLP policies:
    - **Name:** `FSI-RegSP-NPI-Protection-Copilot`
    - **Description:** Protects NPI from unauthorized disclosure through Copilot interactions
    - **Policy admins:** Purview Compliance Admin, Purview Compliance Data Admin, or Purview Data Security AI Admin role group
    - **Location:** Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Chat (Custom policy template; other DLP locations are disabled for that policy)
    - **Explicit experience coverage:** Microsoft 365 Copilot, Copilot Chat, and Copilot in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint. Sensitivity-label rules cover supported stored/open files and emails sent on or after January 1, 2025; calendar invites aren't supported. Use separate workload DLP policies for Exchange, SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams controls outside the documented Copilot path
    - **Sensitive information types for prompt and web-search rules:**
        - US Social Security Number (SSN)
        - US Bank Account Number
        - Credit Card Number
        - US Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)
        - US/UK Passport Number
        - Custom SITs for firm-specific account numbers
    - **Policy actions:**
        - In a separate sensitivity-label rule, use `Prevent Copilot from processing content` for files and emails carrying selected labels. Copilot doesn't use the item's content, although the item can still appear as a citation
        - In a separate SIT rule, use `Prevent Copilot from processing content > Processing prompts` for regulated data typed directly into prompts. This capability is in preview and rolling out, so confirm tenant availability
        - In another SIT rule, use `Prevent Copilot from processing content > Performing Web Searches` to restrict external web search when typed prompts contain NPI SITs; Copilot can still answer from permitted internal sources
        - If the preview has reached the tenant, assess a separate `Email is received from > External users` rule with `Prevent Copilot from processing content`. It evaluates sender-domain metadata, not the email body, and excludes matching external email from grounding, summarization, and citation
    - **Direct-upload limitation:** DLP evaluates text typed into the prompt but doesn't scan the contents of files uploaded directly into prompts. Protect uploaded content with source permissions and sensitivity labels
    - **Web grounding control:** In the [Cloud Policy service for Microsoft 365](https://config.office.com), configure **Allow web search in Copilot** for the approved user/group scope and align it with the DLP web-search rule
    - **Alerts and notifications:** Configure DLP alerts and user notifications according to the approved response process
4. For Security & Compliance PowerShell creation, use the current Copilot location JSON and Copilot enforcement plane rather than workload-specific location switches:

    ```powershell
    Connect-IPPSSession

    $copilotLocation = @'
    [{
      "Workload": "Applications",
      "Location": "470f2276-e011-4e9d-a6ec-20768be3a4b0",
      "Inclusions": [{"Type": "Tenant", "Identity": "All"}]
    }]
    '@

    New-DlpCompliancePolicy `
        -Name "FSI-RegSP-NPI-Protection-Copilot" `
        -Locations $copilotLocation `
        -EnforcementPlanes @("CopilotExperiences") `
        -Mode TestWithoutNotifications
    ```

    The documented PowerShell syntax supports creating the Copilot policy and a sensitivity-label exclusion rule. Configure prompt-processing and web-search rules in the Purview portal unless Microsoft documents the applicable `New-DlpComplianceRule` syntax for the module version in use. Organizations should verify rule conditions and actions against approved labels, SITs, and exception procedures before moving to Enforce mode. Policy updates can take up to four hours to appear in Copilot experiences.

5. Verify capability-specific licensing. DLP prompt safeguards are available to all Microsoft Copilot and Copilot Chat users, subject to feature rollout. Restricting Copilot from processing files and emails requires an eligible Microsoft 365 E5, Microsoft Purview Suite, Information Protection and Governance, or listed Office 365 E5 entitlement.

### Step 3: Implement Sensitivity Labels for NPI Content

1. Create or verify sensitivity labels for NPI-containing content:
    - **Label:** `Confidential - Client NPI`
    - **Protection:** Encryption, prevent forwarding, watermark
    - **Auto-labeling:** Configure auto-labeling rules for documents containing NPI patterns
    - **Copilot behavior:** If a Copilot-location DLP rule targets this label, Copilot doesn't use the labeled file or email content in its response. The item can still appear as a citation
2. Deploy auto-labeling policies for SharePoint sites and OneDrive locations containing client records

### Step 4: Update Privacy Notices

1. Review the firm's current privacy notice under Reg S-P
2. Assess whether AI tool usage is adequately disclosed:
    - Does the notice describe the use of AI tools in processing customer information?
    - Does the notice accurately describe information sharing with technology service providers (Microsoft)?
    - Do opt-out procedures account for AI processing of NPI?
3. Update privacy notices if needed:
    - Add disclosure about AI-assisted processing of customer information
    - Confirm Microsoft is disclosed as a service provider processing NPI
    - Verify opt-out procedures are clear and actionable
4. Distribute updated notices per Reg S-P requirements

### Step 5: Configure Incident Response, Affected-Individual Notice, and Service-Provider Oversight

For Copilot deployments, align the written response program to Rule 248.30(a)(3), affected-individual notification to Rule 248.30(a)(4), and service-provider oversight and provider-to-institution notification to Rule 248.30(a)(5):

1. Document Copilot-related NPI incident scenarios in the written incident response program:
    - **Scenario 1:** Copilot surfaces client NPI to unauthorized user due to permission misconfiguration
    - **Scenario 2:** Copilot-drafted communication includes client NPI that should not have been disclosed
    - **Scenario 3:** User exports NPI from Copilot interaction and shares externally
2. Define incident severity levels:
    - **Critical:** SSN, account credentials, or bulk NPI exposure through Copilot
    - **High:** Individual client financial data exposure through Copilot
    - **Medium:** Client contact information exposure through Copilot
    - **Low:** Near-miss or policy tip triggered without actual NPI disclosure
3. Establish and evidence distinct procedures:
    - **Response program (Rule 248.30(a)(3)):** Assess scope, contain and control the event, recover, and document the institution's response actions.
    - **Service providers (Rule 248.30(a)(5)):** Use due diligence and monitoring, then record the provider's awareness time, the institution's receipt time, the provider-reported scope, and whether receipt met the provider-to-institution 72-hour timing requirement. Do not start this clock from the institution's detection time.
    - **Affected individuals (Rule 248.30(a)(4)):** Document the reasonable investigation and notification decision. When notification is required, track the institution's as-soon-as-practicable, no-later-than-30-day clock separately from the service-provider timing evaluation.
    - The final rule does not create a general written-contract requirement for the provider notice. A provider may be authorized by written agreement to notify affected individuals on the institution's behalf, but the institution remains responsible for that notice.
4. Configure supported Copilot DLP alerts in Microsoft Purview and monitor **Purview > Audit > Copilot activities** for `CopilotInteraction` records
5. For Microsoft-determined service incidents, Microsoft documents notification to designated tenant administrators and, depending on the incident, the Microsoft 365 Service health dashboard. Keep tenant contacts current and monitor Service health; use the institution's approved workflow for customer-detected events

## Financial Sector Considerations

### Reg S-P Amendments Adopted in 2024 (Proposed in 2023): What Changed for Copilot

The key changes relevant to Copilot deployments are now in effect:

- **Response program (Rule 248.30(a)(3)):** Required in written safeguards policies and procedures. Adapt the program to Copilot-related NPI scenarios, including detection, scope assessment, containment, recovery, and notification procedures.
- **Affected-individual notification (Rule 248.30(a)(4)):** Subject to the rule's reasonable-investigation determination, required notice is sent as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days after the institution becomes aware of qualifying unauthorized access or use.
- **Service-provider oversight and notice (Rule 248.30(a)(5)):** Written policies and procedures must use due diligence and monitoring and be reasonably designed to ensure providers protect customer information and notify the institution within 72 hours of the provider becoming aware of a qualifying breach in a provider-maintained customer-information system.
- **No invented contract condition:** The final rule permits a written agreement for a service provider to notify affected individuals for the institution; it does not require a general written contract to establish the provider-to-institution notification duty.

### NPI in Copilot Context Windows

Work-grounded Microsoft Copilot can retrieve permitted emails, files, chats, meetings, and calendar data when responding to a request. It operates in the initiating user's security context; content isn't universally accessible merely because it exists in the tenant. A response that combines NPI from multiple permitted sources can still create a higher-risk exposure scenario than any individual workload alone.

### Opt-Out Considerations

Reg S-P requires that customers be given the opportunity to opt out of NPI sharing with nonaffiliated third parties. While Microsoft is typically a service provider (not a nonaffiliated third party) under GLBA, firms should assess whether:

- Copilot's processing of NPI constitutes "sharing" under the opt-out provisions
- Applicable service-provider exception requirements and oversight are evaluated with counsel; Rule 248.30(a)(5) is not treated as imposing a general written-contract requirement
- Any Copilot extensibility features (plugins, connectors) involve NPI sharing with additional parties

### Intersection with State Privacy Laws

In addition to Reg S-P, state privacy laws may impose additional requirements on NPI processed by Copilot:

- **California CCPA/CPRA:** Right to know, delete, and opt out of sale/sharing of personal information
- **New York DFS Cybersecurity Regulation (23 NYCRR 500):** Cybersecurity program requirements including AI system governance
- **Massachusetts 201 CMR 17.00:** Data security requirements for personal information
- Firms operating in multiple states must maintain a jurisdiction-specific compliance matrix for NPI and AI

### AI-Powered Skill Inferencing and Employee Privacy

!!! info "People Skills inferencing — current behavior"

    AI-powered skill inferencing is on by default for Copilot and Viva users. Admins can also enable inferencing tenant-wide for Microsoft 365 E3/E5 users after People Skills is set up; that expansion is off by default and is available only in tenants with at least one Microsoft Copilot license. Manage it in the Microsoft 365 admin center under **Copilot > Settings > Data access > People Skills in Microsoft Copilot**.

For SEC-regulated entities, AI-powered skill inferencing raises Reg S-P and GLBA employee privacy considerations:

- **Employee NPI processing:** The skill inferencing engine processes work activity data that may include interactions with customer financial information. Organizations should assess whether skill inference processing constitutes a new use of NPI that falls within the scope of privacy notice disclosures.
- **Consent and transparency:** Employees should be informed that their work activity is being analyzed to infer skills. Organizations should evaluate whether existing employee privacy notices and acceptable use policies cover this processing.
- **Recommended posture:** FSI organizations should evaluate whether to disable AI-powered skill updates by default and enable only after a privacy impact assessment is completed. See also [Control 4.4 — Viva Suite Governance](../pillar-4-operations/4.4-viva-suite-governance.md).
